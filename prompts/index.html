THE PROMPTS

Five partner-led challenges. Explore a real-world problem, then build something that makes a difference.

2026 CHALLENGE BRIEFS

Choose a challenge.

Each scenario includes its original poster and full brief. Find the problem your team wants to solve.

01DESNODE

Conquer your day. Make the most of your time.

The challenge

Students often struggle to manage their time, overcome procrastination, stay focused on schoolwork, and develop healthy daily habits. With so many digital distractions, it can be difficult to balance studying, physical activity, personal interests, and time spent outdoors. But what if technology could help students spend their time more meaningfully, achieve their goals, and become more active in the real world? Your team has been challenged to design and build an interactive web application that helps students manage their time and lives better.

02NEUROTX

Capture brainwaves from scalp EEG.

The challenge

Students are challenged to discover how real-time brain data acquisition works and gain a foundational understanding of Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) components. Participants will explore the journey of neural signals from cortical electrical activity to digital commands, learning how hardware electrodes, signal amplifiers, and spatial mapping work together to capture brainwaves.

03DIFY

AI eyes for a better campus.

The challenge

Every day at school, we make small decisions: Which bin should this go in? What does this food label say? Where can I find the right school rule or resource? The information may already exist, but it can be hard to find, read, or understand. AI can now work with both words and images. But seeing a photo is only the beginning. A helpful AI app should also use reliable information, explain when it is unsure, and guide people to the right next step. How could AI that can see, read and understand help people at our school make better everyday choices? Build your solution with Dify.

04STEMHUB

Safe at home, even alone.

The challenge

More and more older people live alone, or spend their days at home alone. The real danger is rarely a serious illness—it is the small things nobody sees: a fall with no one to find them, medicine left untaken, a gas hob left on. Family living away can only check in by phone at fixed times, and when the call goes unanswered, nothing happens. Your task is to design something that keeps an older person safer during the hours they are on their own.

05NYU

Reimagining leisure through technology.

The challenge

Many people spend their leisure time scrolling through their phones, even though they have hobbies they have always wanted to explore. Traditional learning resources usually follow a fixed curriculum, making it difficult for beginners to jump straight into something they love. But what if we could reverse this process? Instead of learning a skill first and applying it later, what if we could start with what we love and learn the necessary skills along the way? How can we use technology to make hobbies more personalized, accessible, and easy to pick up, even for people with no prior experience?
